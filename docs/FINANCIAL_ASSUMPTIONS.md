# Financial assumptions

## Classification of information

| Label | Meaning in this project |
|---|---|
| Synthetic source data | The 5,000 customers and 42,000 transactions generated by the repository. These are not company records. |
| Simulated actual | An observed synthetic transaction evaluated under the legacy credit-score policy. The transaction, fee, and default outcome come from the source file; operating expenses are modeled. |
| Forecast | A forward monthly planning calculation starting after the last source month. |
| Budget | A simulated plan calibrated on the first three complete source months and evaluated against later complete months. |
| Scenario assumption | An editable planning driver, not a measured fact. |

## Core formulas

```text
Approved volume = Origination volume × Approval rate
Merchant fee revenue = Approved volume × Merchant fee rate
Credit losses = Approved volume × Default rate × Loss given default
Processing expense = Approved volume × Processing cost rate
Customer acquisition expense = New active customers × CAC per customer
Servicing expense = Approved transactions × Servicing cost per transaction
Operating expenses = Processing + Acquisition + Servicing + Technology + Other opex
Net revenue = Merchant fee revenue − Credit losses
Operating profit = Net revenue − Operating expenses
Operating margin = Operating profit ÷ Merchant fee revenue
```

Gross transaction or origination volume is never presented as revenue.

## Planning scenarios

| Driver | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Annual transaction growth | -5.0% | 8.0% | 15.0% |
| Annual customer growth | -2.0% | 6.0% | 10.0% |
| Approval rate | 70.0% | 75.0% | 80.0% |
| Merchant fee rate | 3.90% | 4.13% | 4.40% |
| Default rate on approved volume | 8.50% | 6.40% | 5.00% |
| Loss given default | 75.0% | 65.0% | 55.0% |
| Processing cost rate | 0.90% | 0.75% | 0.65% |
| CAC per incremental active customer | $22 | $18 | $16 |
| Servicing cost per approved transaction | $0.90 | $0.75 | $0.65 |
| Monthly technology expense | $42,000 | $35,000 | $33,000 |
| Monthly other operating expense | $18,000 | $15,000 | $14,000 |

The fee-rate and base transaction-volume inputs are anchored to the synthetic portfolio, but every forward driver remains a configurable planning assumption. Flat seasonality is used because the repository has one synthetic year and no defensible multi-year seasonal history.

## Budget methodology

The first three complete synthetic months establish the budget run-rate. The base annual growth assumptions convert to monthly compound rates. Later complete months are held out as simulated actuals. Partial boundary months are excluded from budget evaluation.

Variance attribution reconciles as follows:

```text
Revenue variance = Volume variance + Fee-rate variance
Credit-loss variance = Loss-volume variance + Default/LGD variance
Operating-profit variance = Revenue variance − Credit-loss variance − Operating-cost variance
```

Materiality is the greater of $5,000 or 5% of absolute budgeted operating profit for the month.

## Accounting simplifications

- Merchant fees are recognized when an approved transaction originates.
- Loss is recognized in the modeled month without a delinquency or collections timing curve.
- Funding costs, interest income, late fees, interchange, chargebacks, taxes, depreciation, and working capital are excluded.
- Technology and other operating expenses are fixed monthly planning assumptions.
- The model does not claim GAAP or IFRS financial statements. It is a management P&L for planning analysis.
- The original $149,217 improvement remains a policy backtest result before operating expenses. It is not comparable to forecast operating profit without this distinction.

## Limitations

- The data is synthetic and transaction dates are relative to the date on which the generator was run.
- Only one synthetic year is available, so the model does not infer seasonality.
- Approval rate and default rate are independently configurable in scenarios. A production plan would model their causal relationship from policy curves.
- LGD remains assumed rather than measured.
- Operating costs are not sourced from a general ledger.
